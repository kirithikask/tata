COOLING SYSTEM - STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE
Excavator Hydraulic Oil Cooler - Inspection and Service

1. PURPOSE
This SOP describes standard steps for inspecting and servicing the
hydraulic oil cooler on excavators, written for the Engineering Memory
prototype as demo reference data.

2. WHEN TO INSPECT
Inspect the cooler when oil temperature readings trend higher than normal,
when hydraulic performance degrades after extended operating periods (a
pattern often described as "fine in the morning, slow by afternoon"), or at
the standard scheduled service interval.

3. SAFETY PREPARATION
Shut down the engine and allow the cooler and surrounding components to
cool before inspection. Wear eye protection when using compressed air or a
pressure washer near the cooler fins.

4. VISUAL INSPECTION
Step 1: Locate the oil cooler, typically mounted near the radiator.
Step 2: Visually inspect the fins for dirt, mud, or debris buildup.
Step 3: Check for bent or damaged fins that would restrict airflow.
Step 4: Inspect the cooling fan belt for wear, cracking, or looseness.

5. CLEANING PROCEDURE
Step 1: Use low-pressure compressed air or a pressure washer, directed
opposite to normal airflow, to clear debris from the fins.
Step 2: Avoid bending fins during cleaning; work in small sections.
Step 3: Inspect fan belt tension and adjust or replace if loose or worn.
Step 4: Confirm the cooling fan spins freely with no obstruction.

6. POST-SERVICE CHECKS
After cleaning, run the machine under normal working load for at least 30
minutes and monitor oil temperature. Confirm temperature stabilizes within
the normal operating range rather than continuing to climb.

7. RELATED SYMPTOMS
Cooler restriction typically presents as performance that is normal when
the machine is cool but degrades progressively as operating time and oil
temperature increase. This pattern overlaps with pump wear, so oil
temperature trend should always be checked before assuming a pump fault.
