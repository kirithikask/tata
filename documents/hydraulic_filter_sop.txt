HYDRAULIC FILTER - STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE
Excavator Hydraulic System - Filter Inspection and Replacement

1. PURPOSE
This SOP describes the standard steps for inspecting and replacing hydraulic
filters (return line filter and suction strainer) on excavators, written for
the Engineering Memory prototype as demo reference data.

2. WHEN TO INSPECT
Inspect filters when the bypass indicator is triggered, when jerky or
inconsistent hydraulic response is reported, after any major hydraulic
component repair, or at the standard scheduled service interval.

3. SAFETY PREPARATION
Shut down the engine and relieve system pressure before opening any filter
housing. Allow hot oil to cool to a safe handling temperature. Have a drain
pan ready to catch residual oil.

4. RETURN LINE FILTER REPLACEMENT
Step 1: Locate the return line filter housing near the hydraulic tank.
Step 2: Clean the exterior of the housing before opening to prevent debris
entry.
Step 3: Remove the housing cap and extract the filter element.
Step 4: Inspect the old element for metal particles, which may indicate
pump or component wear upstream.
Step 5: Install a new filter element of the correct part specification.
Step 6: Reassemble the housing and torque to specification.
Step 7: Start the engine and check for leaks at the housing.

5. SUCTION STRAINER CLEANING
Step 1: Drain the hydraulic tank to below the strainer level.
Step 2: Remove the strainer access panel.
Step 3: Extract and clean the strainer mesh using an approved solvent.
Step 4: Inspect the mesh for tears or damage; replace if compromised.
Step 5: Reinstall the strainer and access panel.
Step 6: Refill the tank to the correct oil level.

6. POST-SERVICE CHECKS
After any filter service, run the machine through a full range of
functions (boom, arm, bucket, swing) and confirm smooth, consistent
response with no unusual noise. Recheck the bypass indicator after a short
operating period to confirm it has cleared.

7. RELATED SYMPTOMS
Filter blockage typically presents as inconsistent or jerky movement across
functions rather than a gradual, uniform power loss. If symptoms persist
after filter replacement, investigate pump or valve causes next.
