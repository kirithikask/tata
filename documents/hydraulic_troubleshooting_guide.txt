HYDRAULIC TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE
Excavator Hydraulic System - General Reference

1. PURPOSE
This guide provides a general reference for diagnosing slow or weak hydraulic
performance on excavators. It is written for the Engineering Memory prototype
and reflects common, publicly known hydraulic fault patterns. It does not
represent official Tata Hitachi service documentation and should not be used
for real maintenance decisions.

2. COMMON SYMPTOM: SLOW OR WEAK OPERATION
When boom, arm, or bucket movement becomes slow or loses digging force, the
root cause usually falls into one of the following categories: pump wear,
filter or line restriction, oil condition, valve sticking, accumulator
pressure loss, cylinder internal leakage, or pilot pressure problems.

3. PUMP-RELATED CAUSES
Internal pump leakage occurs when pump seals or wear plates degrade,
allowing oil to bypass internally instead of building pressure. This is more
noticeable under load and often worsens as oil temperature rises, since hot
oil is thinner and bypasses more easily. Symptom pattern: gradual power
loss across all functions, worse when hot.

4. COOLING-RELATED CAUSES
A restricted oil cooler reduces the system's ability to shed heat. As oil
temperature climbs, viscosity drops and internal leakage increases across
every component, which is why cooler restriction often looks identical to
pump wear at first glance. Check cooler fins for debris and confirm the
cooling fan is operating before assuming a pump fault.

5. FILTER AND LINE RESTRICTION
A clogged return filter or suction strainer restricts oil flow, causing
jerky or inconsistent movement rather than a smooth power loss. This
symptom pattern (inconsistent, not gradual) helps distinguish filter issues
from pump wear. Check filter bypass indicators first, since this is the
fastest and cheapest check to rule out.

6. VALVE PROBLEMS
A sticking spool valve, often caused by varnish buildup in older or
contaminated oil, causes uneven response between functions. One function
may work normally while another lags. This asymmetry across functions is
the key distinguishing sign versus a system-wide pump or cooler issue.

7. ACCUMULATOR PRESSURE LOSS
Low accumulator pre-charge pressure causes a delayed or jerky start when a
function is first engaged, but normal operation once running. This
"jerky start, smooth after" pattern is distinct from a continuous slowness
pattern.

8. OIL CONTAMINATION
Particulate or water contamination in the hydraulic oil affects every
function simultaneously and often correlates with unusual noise. An oil
sample analysis is the most reliable way to confirm this versus assuming a
mechanical fault.

9. CYLINDER INTERNAL LEAKAGE
A cylinder with a worn piston seal will drift down under load or lose force
in one specific function while others remain normal. This is similar in
presentation to a valve problem but is isolated to a single actuator rather
than a control path.

10. PILOT PRESSURE PROBLEMS
Low pilot pressure causes levers to feel "light" or unresponsive across
multiple functions, distinct from a hydraulic power loss because the main
pump and valves may test normally. Check the pilot pressure reducing valve
and pilot filter first.

11. RECOMMENDED DIAGNOSTIC ORDER
Check the cheapest and fastest items first: filter bypass indicator, oil
level and visible contamination, cooler fin cleanliness. Then check
pressure readings (main and pilot). Only after ruling out these should
pump or cylinder teardown be considered, since these are the most costly
to repair.
